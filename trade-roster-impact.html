Trade & Roster Impact

Big offseason moves change how good a team really is, but the early numbers do not always match the headlines. Here is how three of the biggest storylines look in the data as of November 12, 2026.

LeBron James joins the Philadelphia 76ers

LeBron James signed a two-year deal with the 76ers this offseason. Philadelphia is 9-3, second in the East, which looks great on paper. The underlying numbers are less convincing: their net rating is only +1.0, their SRS is -2.86, and ESPN’s BPI ranks them 13th in the league. That gap suggests the record may be ahead of the team’s actual performance, or that the roster is still coming together.

The Spurs build around Victor Wembanyama

Victor Wembanyama leads the league in win shares at 2.6, and the Spurs are 10-2 with the top spot in ESPN’s BPI at 9.5. Their net rating of +11.2 backs up the record. Of the teams I am tracking, San Antonio is the one where the record, the BPI, and the net rating all point in the same direction.

Giannis Antetokounmpo moves to the Miami Heat

Giannis Antetokounmpo is now with the Heat, who are 5-6 with a net rating of -4.1 and a BPI rank of 16th. His former team, the Milwaukee Bucks, are 1-11. Neither team has had the start it hoped for, which shows that adding or losing a star does not guarantee a result.
